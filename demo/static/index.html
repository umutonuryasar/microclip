MicroCLIP

A CLIP-style image–text model built from scratch on a single-GPU budget: the tokenizer, both encoders, the attention, the two contrastive losses, the training loop and the evaluation are all hand-written, with no pretrained weights. Search 5,000 COCO images by description. Every query runs through two models that differ only in their loss function, which is the experiment this project was built to run.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no server.

How it works, and how well

The pipeline

Your text is split by a 16K byte-level BPE tokenizer trained on COCO captions, re-implemented here in JavaScript and checked against the Python original on thousands of captions. A 4-layer Transformer encoder, written by hand rather than with a framework attention module, reads those tokens, and the hidden state at the end-of-sequence position becomes the sentence vector. The images went through a ResNet-18 trained from random initialization. Both sides project into the same 256-dimensional space and are L2-normalized, so cosine similarity ranks them.

Image embeddings were computed offline and ship with the page, so a search is one text-encoder pass plus a dot product against 5,000 vectors. The encoders run as int8-quantized ONNX graphs, which match the full-precision models on 99% of top-1 results at a quarter of the download size.

The experiment

SigLIP reports that its sigmoid loss beats softmax at small batch sizes. Tested here at small scale, with identical architecture and data, 3 seeds and batch sizes from 128 to 512, softmax won at every batch size, and its lead grew as the batch shrank, the opposite of the hypothesis. That is a non-replication at small scale, not a refutation: SigLIP's advantage is reported at far larger batch and data scales than anything here.

What to expect

Absolute quality is modest by design. This is a small model trained from scratch on 118K images, not a production retrieval system. Roughly half of image→text queries land a correct match in the top 10. It has only ever seen COCO, so COCO-like scenes work best and unusual queries return loose matches. Scores shown under each image are cosine similarities, followed by that image's ground-truth COCO caption.
